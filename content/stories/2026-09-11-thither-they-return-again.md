4) 
Timothy Tufts Benton drove slower and rolled down both windows more. Yesterday was with relentless summery sunblaze, yet today, at the same hours, autumnal undersweep. Cloud as thick and rusty as boat decks, over the leftover stubborn greeneries and crumpy-skinned workfolks, glooms. Gracing down through broken cloud holes, one in a couple hundred square miles, is what remains of the cleansing sunray.

The sea is eternal. Regardless of how many times a person descends to it, it is there, vast and stoic. It orchestrates its tides into the indefinite crusade against land and whom it bears. Its unforgiving salty breath makes enemies of all, in its way. Surreptitiously it disguises itself as the colored background unity whereon mortals play their roles and struggle.

The actors and props. temporal.

A long weekend, today a kayaker pushed his measly vessel onto the blue watery stage. He paddled and navigated carefully around the phalanx of masts and antennas of the parked boats. Within an hour he went far. The orange plastic piece emerged from the wavepeaks momentarily before disappearing into the unmeasurable depths between. The Atlantic push rebounded off the shoreline and easily manipulated his fate. In the grand scheme of things, generously assuming he would make his planned return, no visible change could be observed on the eternal blue, however hard the man claimed an overcoming of natural brutality.

Here he came, pulling himself up the ramp, whose end points to THE MOTIF. Sitting on a wharf, off an underwhelming single-level hut with a modest lookout tower, beetling out its introspective corroded burgundy paint, is a palette of lobster buoys, coral-reef-colored tiramisu.

Liz Benton stared at the motif and the kayaker for a while.
